# Research method for Responsible AI foundations

Counts are OpenAlex `cited_by_count` as of 2026-10-08; they under-count arXiv papers. Treat them as a relative ranking, never a true total. Where OpenAlex lists both the arXiv record and the venue record of one paper, the venue record (higher count) was kept. Semantic Scholar was not used (unauthenticated calls answer 429). Tier is from one batched `source-checker` call over all 41 rows: PRIMARY read the source or its official abstract, SECONDARY confirmed through a reputable citing source, UNVERIFIED not reached, CONTRADICTED the source says otherwise, SUPERSEDED replaced by a newer document.

## Dropped and why

- "Data validation for machine learning production" query returned nothing on the topic (generic ML surveys); Breck 2019 and Polyzotis 2017 appear only as "Data Management Challenges in Production Machine Learning" (221) and were not needed once Hort, Gender Shades and Obermeyer covered kp6 support.
- "Concrete Problems in AI Safety" (Amodei 2016) did not surface under its title; left out rather than cited with a remembered count.
- "Fairness and Machine Learning: Limitations and Opportunities" (Barocas, Hardt, Narayanan) did not surface in OpenAlex; "Weapons of Math Destruction" took the practitioner slot instead.
- "The Measure and Mismeasure of Fairness" (Corbett-Davies and Goel) is in OpenAlex only as the 2018 arXiv record (464); Kearns 2017 took the kp6 extends slot because subgroup auditing is closer to the course's "Subgroup analysis" lesson. Kearns too is the arXiv record; the ICML 2018 record did not surface.
- "Weapons of Math Destruction": OpenAlex holds several records with the book's title that are reviews in journals (The Information Society 3122, Business Economics 1079); the book record itself (1938, no DOI) was kept with the publisher page as url, so the count is the book's.
- Solove 2013: OpenAlex has the Harvard Law Review record (425) titled "Introduction: Privacy Self-Management and the Consent Dilemma" and a GWU repository record (306); the Harvard Law Review record was kept with the GWU open-access page as the url.
- Gender Shades has no DOI in OpenAlex; url is the PMLR page.
- The EU AI Act and ISO/IEC 42001 are not indexed as works in OpenAlex (the queries return commentary); both carry `cited_by` 0 with "no count" in why.
- NIST IR 8286 (2020, DOI 10.6028/nist.ir.8286, OpenAlex count 51) was tiered SUPERSEDED by source-checker: withdrawn 2025-12-18 and replaced by IR 8286 Rev. 1 (December 2025). The row now cites Rev. 1 (DOI 10.6028/nist.ir.8286r1, Crossref) with `cited_by` 0, no count found for the revision.
- The EU AI Act row was read in the browser pane on eur-lex (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, recital 26, Articles 6, 9 and 12), but eur-lex answers 202 with an empty body to fetch tools, so source-checker confirmed it on the artificialintelligenceact.eu mirror and tiered it SECONDARY; the tier is kept as the checker gave it. The checker also flagged that the Act was amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI, in force 27 July 2026), which moved the high-risk application dates; the why line says so.
- ISO/IEC 42001 was read on the iso.org catalogue page (curl with a browser user agent; the page answers 403 to fetch tools), so source-checker confirmed it through the IEC webstore and tiered it SECONDARY.
- The two NIST DOIs answer 404 to an HTTP HEAD (what `check_links.py` sends) but 200 with the PDF to a GET; the rows' urls point at the nist.gov and csrc.nist.gov landing pages, which answer 200 to HEAD, and the DOIs are kept.
- Em dashes inside two OpenAlex titles (Veale and Zuiderveen Borgesius; AI4People) were replaced with hyphens before scaffolding so that `new_entry.py` reports zero replacements.

## Course bundle method

One course per platform, the source track and its four member courses excluded. Each pick was confirmed on its own page on 2026-10-08: Coursera, DataCamp, 365 Data Science and DeepLearning.AI by WebFetch; Udemy answers 403 to fetch tools, so its page was read in the browser pane (title, 3h 41m, 13 sections, "No technical background required"). 365 Data Science has no AI ethics, governance or regulation course beyond "ChatGPT at Work: Ethical and Secure ChatGPT Use", which already has its own entry in this DB; it was kept as the platform's slot because its sections on the core principles of AI ethics and on GDPR, the EU AI Act and US state rules are relevant. Nothing was taken from a search snippet alone.
